What... do those underlined words mean?

Definition

👨 Scante Arrhenius, one of the pioneers of physical chemistry described acids and bases as the following:

✔️ Arrhenius Acid: When you add an acid into water, it breaks apart into H+ and a anion.

✔️ Arrhenius Base: When you add a base into water, it breaks apart into OH- and a cation.

💡 This is an interesting property of acids and base: that they break apart in water, this is called dissosciation or ionization!
Blood is basic, however, not all of its components release a free H+ into the system!
👥 Brønsted and Lowry realized that not all bases and acids release a H+ or OH- when they dissociate. So they made a more general version of the theory of Arrhenius:

💓 Brønsted-Lowry Acid: An acid is a proton donor.

💓 Brønsted-Lowry Base: A base is a proton reciever.

This is implies the existence of secondary compounds that recieve the protons of the acid and give protons to the base. These are called conjugates!
So an acid has a conjugate base, and a base has a conjugate acid!

Examples:

HCl is mixed into some water, and it dissociates into H+ and Cl-. Is it a base or an acid?

According to the Arrhenius definition, this is an acid because it realeses H+ and an anion! 😄😃

Later on, the H+ in the water got absorbed by other water molecules, which formed H3O+. Is HCl still an acid, or is it a base?

Yes, it still is! According to the Brønsted-Lowry definition, an acid is a proton donor! And the conjugate base in this scenario is H3O+! 😄🌟✊
Nice one gang! 😄
✨ Something spectacular occurs naturally in water called autoionization!

Water breaks down into H+ and OH- randomly by itself! And the other water molecules absorb the H+ and OH- to form H3O+ and H2O2! Can you explain how water acts as a base and acid using the Arrhenius and Brønsted-Lowry definitions?

According to the Arrhenius definition, water is both an acid and base because it releases both H+ and OH-.

According to the Brønsted-Lowry definition definition, water is both an acid and base because it can donate a proton and recieve it too.
